Respiratory system

COPD

Indications for pulmonary rehabilitation, assessment of exercise tolerance, aerobic and strength programs, monitoring of desaturation, and nutritional risk.

01 · Starting point

Baseline data before prescribing exercise

ParameterWhat to assess
Clinical statusmMRC (modified Medical Research Council dyspnea scale), CAT (COPD Assessment Test), cough/sputum, number of exacerbations and hospitalizations, current stability
Lung functionPost-bronchodilator spirometry; when indicated, DLCO (diffusing capacity of the lungs for carbon monoxide) and lung volumes
Gas exchangeSpO₂ at rest and during exercise; blood gases when indicated; prescribed oxygen therapy and target range
Exercise capacity6MWT (6-minute walk test), ISWT/ESWT (incremental / endurance shuttle walk test), or CPET (cardiopulmonary exercise test); Borg dyspnea and fatigue ratings; speed/power that causes desaturation
Muscle function1RM (one-repetition maximum)/multiple-repetition test, grip strength, 5 chair stands, signs of sarcopenia
NutritionWeight, BMI (body mass index), weight trend, FFMI (fat-free mass index) if available, MUST/GLIM (malnutrition screening / Global Leadership Initiative on Malnutrition criteria) or local screening
Treatment and techniqueBronchodilators, ICS (inhaled corticosteroids) when indicated, inhaler technique, oxygen, NIV (non-invasive ventilation), smoking
ComorbiditiesCoronary artery disease/heart failure, pulmonary hypertension, osteoporosis, anemia, anxiety/depression, balance impairment

02 · Triage

Triage before starting training

SituationAction
Stable symptomatic COPDReferral to pulmonary rehabilitation — strong ATS (American Thoracic Society) recommendation
After hospitalization for an exacerbationReferral to pulmonary rehabilitation after stabilization — strong ATS recommendation
Stable condition and remote format availableA choice between an in-person program and telerehabilitation is acceptable when safe assessment and monitoring are in place
Current exacerbation, fever, increased sputum/shortness of breath, new chest pain, decompensated heart failurePostpone planned exercise; clinical assessment
Unexplained severe desaturation, syncope, suspected pulmonary hypertension/ischemiaFurther evaluation before an independent program

03 · Aerobic exercise

Aerobic exercise parameters

ParameterPrescription
Frequency3–5 days per week
Duration20–60 minutes; an interval scheme is acceptable with marked shortness of breath
IntensityBased on CPET/field test; working target — Borg dyspnea 4–6/10 and fatigue 4–6/10 with controlled symptoms
Continuous modeWalking or cycling at the highest sustainable tolerated power
Interval modeShort work bouts with active or passive recovery if continuous work is limited by shortness of breath/dynamic hyperinflation
ProgressionTotal time first, then work rate; change only one parameter per stage

04 · Strength training

Strength and breathing training

ComponentPrescription
Strength work2–3 days/week; 1–3 sets of 8–12 repetitions; start at roughly 50–60% 1RM, progressing to 60–80% while maintaining technique
With marked shortness of breathTrain small muscle groups separately, include more pauses, use seated exercises, prioritize strength work as it is less ventilatory-demanding
Inspiratory muscle trainingConsider for documented respiratory muscle weakness and persistent shortness of breath as an adjunct, not a replacement for general rehabilitation
Breathing techniquePursed-lip exhalation and pacing control — to reduce dynamic hyperinflation in patients who benefit from it

05 · Oxygen

Oxygen and monitoring

  • Oxygen during exercise is used only as prescribed; do not change the flow/mode on your own.
  • Do not set a universal SpO₂ target: use the range set by the physician, taking the risk of hypercapnia into account.
  • An SpO₂ drop below the individual threshold, marked desaturation, or a need for increased oxygen requires stopping/reducing exercise and clinical reassessment.
  • Monitor SpO₂, heart rate, Borg dyspnea and fatigue ratings, symptoms, and recovery time; with diabetes, monitor glucose according to the individual plan.

06 · Nutrition

Nutrition parameters

SituationApproach
Weight loss/low BMI/reduced FFMIRule out causes of catabolism; energy-enriched diet; protein usually 1.2–1.5 g/kg/day in illness or malnutrition, individualized by kidney function and overall condition
Early satiety/shortness of breath while eatingSmall, more frequent meals; separate the main exercise session and meals in time
ObesityModerate energy deficit with mandatory strength training and sufficient protein; avoid rapid loss of lean mass
MicronutrientsCorrect only a confirmed deficiency or an established indication

07 · Safety

Stopping exercise

Stop training if you experience:

  • chest pain;
  • presyncope/syncope;
  • confusion;
  • cyanosis;
  • new arrhythmia;
  • hemoptysis;
  • a sudden worsening of shortness of breath;
  • inability to recover after reducing the work rate;
  • marked desaturation relative to the individual tolerance;
  • signs of an exacerbation.
