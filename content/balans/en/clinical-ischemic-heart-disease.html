02 · Routing

Routing before starting training

Level 1

Do not start exercise

  • unstable angina or angina at rest;
  • early unstable ACS phase;
  • decompensated heart failure;
  • uncontrolled life-threatening arrhythmia;
  • significant ischemia at less than 2 MET or less than 50 W;
  • acute myocarditis or pericarditis;
  • acute systemic illness.
Level 2

Controlled cardiac rehabilitation only

  • low ischemic threshold;
  • complex ventricular arrhythmias;
  • fall in systolic BP with increasing load;
  • reduced LV function;
  • chronotropic insufficiency;
  • ICD present;
  • recent MI, PCI or CABG;
  • multiple significant comorbidities.
Level 3

Individual program

Stable clinical condition after evaluation of symptoms, LV function, residual ischemia, arrhythmias, and response to exercise testing.

Note. This is a practical routing algorithm, not a formal cardiovascular risk score.

04 · Intensity

Intensity control

When CPET is available

CPET — cardiopulmonary exercise test with gas exchange. Ventilatory thresholds and corresponding HR, power, speed, and oxygen uptake are determined.

ZonePhysiological featurePractical use
Below VT1 Light, mostly aerobic load that can be sustained long Warm-up, recovery, rehab start, low exercise tolerance
VT1–VT2 Moderate or moderately high steady load Main zone for sustained aerobic training
Above VT2 Rapid metabolic acidosis, sharp rise in ventilation, limited work duration Short intervals after clinical risk assessment, mainly supervised

VT1 — first ventilatory threshold.

VT2 — second ventilatory threshold, or respiratory compensation point.

Intensity is set from CPET-derived HR and power/speed values, not abstractly “by thresholds”.

Practical example

If on CPET:

  • VT1 is 110 bpm and 75 W;
  • VT2 is 135 bpm and 125 W,

then:

  • starting load — up to 110 bpm or 75 W;
  • main sustained work — 110–135 bpm or 75–125 W;
  • work above 135 bpm or 125 W — only short intervals after risk assessment.

Ischemic threshold limit

Upper training heart rate should be at least 10 bpm below the HR at which ischemia or angina appears on exercise testing.

If ischemia appears at 125 bpm, upper training limit is about 115 bpm even if VT2 is higher.

The lowest limit takes priority:

  1. ischemic threshold minus 10 bpm;
  2. upper bound of chosen training zone;
  3. threshold for clinically significant arrhythmia or abnormal BP response.

Exercise test without gas analysis

Target HR = resting HR + 40–60% × (max HR − resting HR)
After adaptation, range may increase to 60–80% of HR reserve.
Use peak HR from the exercise test, not an age-predicted formula.

β-blockers, ivabradine, and chronotropic insufficiency

HR alone is insufficient. Use:

  • power or speed from exercise testing;
  • Borg 6–20 scale;
  • symptoms and exercise tolerance.

For sustained aerobic work:

  • starting range — Borg 11–13;
  • after adaptation — Borg 13–15.

If exercise testing was not done

Before testing:

  • light or moderate intensity only;
  • Borg 9–12;
  • ability to speak in full sentences;
  • no angina, abnormal breathlessness, dizziness, or rhythm disturbance.
Limit. High-intensity and interval training are not prescribed without prior assessment.

07 · Monitoring

Training monitoring

Before exercise

Before starting

  • symptoms;
  • BP and HR;
  • therapy changes;
  • previous session tolerance;
  • weight gain over 1.8 kg in 1–3 days;
  • edema or worsening breathlessness.
During exercise

During session

  • angina and equivalents;
  • breathlessness;
  • RPE;
  • Heart rate;
  • HELL;
  • ECG when higher risk.
After exercise

Recovery

  • HR and BP recovery;
  • persistent symptoms;
  • rhythm disturbances.
When indicated

Additionally

  • glucose in diabetes;
  • SpO₂ in heart failure, COPD, or other clinical need.

09 · Safety

Algorithm for stopping exercise

  1. Stop exercise immediately.
  2. Assess consciousness, symptoms, HR and BP; ECG and SpO₂ if available.
  3. For angina, use individually prescribed short-acting nitrate plan.
  4. Call emergency services for ongoing pain, suspected ACS, syncope, hemodynamic instability, severe arrhythmia, or neurological symptoms.
  5. Do not resume training the same day after angina, abnormal BP response, or arrhythmia.
  6. Before the next session, reassess clinical status, therapy, and training zones.
