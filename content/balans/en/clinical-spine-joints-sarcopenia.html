Musculoskeletal system

Back, joints, and sarcopenia

Three modules: chronic low back pain, osteoarthritis, and sarcopenia. Shared content covers red-flag screening and functional assessment.

Baseline assessment Red flags Low back pain Osteoarthritis Sarcopenia Training Nutrition Stopping exercise Sources

01 · Baseline

Shared baseline assessment

ParameterWhat to establish
SymptomsLocation, duration, intensity, night pain, morning stiffness, neurological symptoms, joint swelling
FunctionWalking, stairs, sit-to-stand, ADL; five sit-to-stands, TUG, gait speed, SPPB
Strength and muscle massHand grip, 1RM/multirep test, circumferences/bioimpedance/DXA when indicated
Fall riskFalls in past year, balance, vision, orthostasis, medicines, home environment
ComorbiditiesOsteoporosis, inflammatory disease, obesity, diabetes, CKD, cardiovascular risk, cancer history
NutritionWeight and trend, protein/energy intake, teeth/swallowing, vitamin D deficiency when indicated

02 · Red flags

Signs needing urgent routing

ConditionSigns
SpineNew urinary/fecal retention or incontinence, saddle anesthesia, progressive motor deficit, significant trauma/suspected fracture, fever/immunosuppression, cancer history with new pain
JointAcute hot swollen joint, fever, inability to bear weight after injury, suspected infection or fracture
GeneralRapid unexplained weight loss, severe night pain with systemic features, abrupt functional decline
Note. Without red flags, routine imaging for nonspecific low back pain is not a prerequisite for starting active recovery.

03 · Low back

Chronic low back pain

ComponentPrescription
Core strategyStructured individualized exercise program; prolonged bed rest is not recommended
Frequency2–3 targeted sessions per week plus regular daily activity
ContentGeneral strength, endurance, trunk work, aerobic load; choice by tolerance and goals
DosingStart with tolerable volume; brief pain increase may be acceptable without new neurological deficit or sustained functional decline
ProgressionReps/time → resistance/complexity → specific work and daily tasks
Principle. Do not claim one exercise system is superior for all patients. Manual and passive methods may supplement but not replace an active program.

04 · Osteoarthritis

Osteoarthritis of major joints

ComponentPrescription
Aerobic loadUp to 150 minutes moderate activity per week including low-impact options; start with short bouts
Strength work2–3 days/week; 1–3 sets of 8–15 reps for muscles around affected joint and major groups
Neuromuscular workBalance, coordination, limb alignment control, functional movement technique
Range of motionRegular work in tolerable range without forcing through acute pain
Body weightIf overweight — at least 5% loss; symptom benefit increases with greater safe loss
Response rule. If pain and swelling worsen substantially and do not return to baseline by the next day, reduce volume/intensity or change load type.

05 · Sarcopenia

Screening and confirmation (EWGSOP2)

EWGSOP2 — European Working Group on Sarcopenia in Older People, second edition criteria.

EWGSOP2 stageThreshold
Low hand grip strength<27 kg men; <16 kg women
Five sit-to-stand test>15 seconds
Low appendicular muscle mass/height²<7.0 kg/m² men; <5.5 kg/m² women
Low gait speed≤0.8 m/s
Low SPPB≤8 points
Logic. Low muscle strength makes sarcopenia probable; low muscle mass confirms; low physical performance indicates severe sarcopenia.

06 · Training

Training with sarcopenia

ParameterPrescription
Frequency2–3 days per week, not consecutive
Volume2–3 sets on main movements
RepetitionsUsually 6–12; in weak patients start with higher reps and lower resistance
IntensityStart around 40–50% 1RM; progress to 60–80% 1RM with good technique
PriorityKnee/hip extensors, pull and push patterns, sit-to-stand, carries, stairs
PowerAfter technique — faster concentric phase with light/moderate load under control
Balance2–3 days/week or more with high fall risk

07 · Nutrition

Nutrition with sarcopenia and in older age

ParameterReference
EnergyAbout 30 kcal/kg/day as starting estimate adjusted for age, activity, disease, and weight trend
Protein: healthy older adult1.0–1.2 g/kg/day
Protein: acute/chronic illness1.2–1.5 g/kg/day; individualize for kidney function and tolerance
Severe illness/injury/malnutritionNeeds may reach 2.0 g/kg/day only within a clinical nutrition plan
FiberAbout 25 g/day with adequate hydration and tolerance
Weight lossIn older adults — only with obesity and related problems, together with strength work and adequate protein

08 · Safety

Stopping exercise

Stop training for new neurological deficit, pelvic organ dysfunction, acute injury, hot swollen joint, inability to bear weight, chest pain, syncope, or abrupt functional decline.

Differentiate ordinary delayed-onset muscle soreness from worsening joint, radicular, or night pain.
