Respiratory system

Asthma

Disease control, prevention of bronchoconstriction, aerobic and strength training parameters, symptom monitoring, and criteria for stopping exercise.

01 · Starting point

Baseline data before prescribing exercise

ParameterWhat to assess
Control over the past 4 weeksDaytime symptoms more than twice a week; night-time awakenings; for patients who relieve symptoms with a SABA (short-acting β₂-agonist), use more than twice a week, not counting preventive use before exercise; activity limitation
Exacerbation riskExacerbations and hospitalizations in the past year; systemic corticosteroids (glucocorticoids); excessive SABA use; absent or insufficient adherence to ICS (inhaled corticosteroids); smoking/vaping; exposure to allergens and air pollution
Lung functionSpirometry with a bronchodilator test; personal best PEF (peak expiratory flow) if a diary is available
Exercise-related symptomsTiming of cough, wheezing, chest tightness, or shortness of breath; relation to cold/dry air, allergens, infection
TreatmentICS-containing therapy; inhaler technique; adherence; written action plan; medication prescribed before exercise
ComorbiditiesAllergic rhinitis, obesity, GERD (gastroesophageal reflux disease), dysfunctional breathing, anxiety, cardiovascular disease
Exercise assessmentSymptom-limited test, CPET (cardiopulmonary exercise test), or field test when the cause of shortness of breath is unclear or symptoms do not match lung function

03 · Aerobic exercise

Aerobic exercise parameters

ParameterPrescription
Frequency3–5 days per week
Duration20–60 minutes; with low tolerance, 5–10-minute intervals
IntensityModerate: Borg 11–13/20 or able to speak in phrases; with CPET, mostly below VT2 (second ventilatory threshold), starting below/around VT1 (first ventilatory threshold)
TypeWalking, cycling, swimming if the environment is tolerated, running, games, and other cyclical activities
Warm-up10–15 minutes with a gradual increase in intensity
ProgressionDuration and frequency first, then intensity
Principle. Regular activity is recommended. Exercise is not ruled out simply because it provokes symptoms: first optimize asthma control and prevention of exercise-induced bronchoconstriction.

04 · Strength training

Strength training

ParameterPrescription
Frequency2–3 days per week, non-consecutive
Volume1–3 sets for the major muscle groups
Repetitions8–15 with maintained technique and without straining
IntensityModerate; target Borg 11–14/20
BreathingExhale during the concentric phase; avoid breath-holding

05 · Prevention

Algorithm for preventing exercise-related symptoms

  1. Check current control, adherence to ICS-containing therapy, and inhaler technique.
  2. Check that a written plan exists and that the prescribed reliever medication is available.
  3. If the individual plan includes a pre-exercise medication, use it only as prescribed. ICS-formoterol may be used before exercise in patients who have been prescribed it as a reliever.
  4. Perform a gradual warm-up; in cold, dry air, reduce intensity, use a warmer/more humid environment, or protect the airways.
  5. If symptoms occur, reduce or stop exercise, use the prescribed medication, and assess the response.
  6. Recurrent symptoms despite prevention are a reason to reassess control and the diagnosis, not to restrict activity permanently.

06 · Nutrition

Nutrition parameters

SituationApproach
Overweight/obesityA weight-loss goal of 5–10% may improve asthma control; the energy deficit is individualized, preserving protein and lean mass
Normal body weightA balanced eating pattern without special “anti-asthma” restrictions
Elimination dietsOnly for confirmed food allergy or another established indication
SupplementsRoutine use of vitamins, antioxidants, omega-3, or magnesium for asthma control does not replace baseline therapy

07 · Monitoring

Monitoring and stopping exercise

Monitor symptoms, Borg rating, heart rate and, when indicated, SpO₂ and PEF before/after exercise.

Stop exercise if

  • shortness of breath increases rapidly;
  • you are unable to speak;
  • marked wheezing occurs;
  • cyanosis appears;
  • chest pain occurs;
  • presyncope/syncope occurs;
  • SpO₂ drops;
  • the prescribed medication does not give a rapid effect.
Severe attack. Follow the written plan and call emergency services.
