Recovery

After heart attack and stroke

Two separate rehabilitation routes: after myocardial infarction and after stroke. Shared content covers secondary prevention, nutrition, and long-term monitoring.

01 · Route choice

Choosing a rehabilitation route

EventPrimary route
Myocardial infarction/ACS, PCI, CABGCardiac rehabilitation; ischemia, LV function, arrhythmias, hemodynamics. Detailed intensity — on the ischemic heart disease page
Ischemic or hemorrhagic strokeNeurological rehabilitation with assessment of function, swallowing, cognition, balance, fall risk, and cardiovascular tolerance
Stroke and IHD combinedJoint limit by higher risk; exercise prescribed to the minimum safe ceiling

02 · After MI

After myocardial infarction: baseline data

ParameterWhat to establish
Event and interventionDate of ACS/MI, PCI/CABG, complications, residual ischemia, completeness of revascularization
Heart functionLV ejection fraction, heart failure, valvular disease
Rhythm and hemodynamicsECG, clinically significant arrhythmias, blood pressure response, orthostasis
Exercise testingCPET or symptom-limited test; ischemic and arrhythmic thresholds
Treatmentβ-blockers, antianginals, anticoagulants/antiplatelets, antihypertensive therapy

Routing and timing

  • After stabilization, refer to cardiac rehabilitation without undue delay.
  • After ACS, start outpatient program ideally within 14 days and no later than 30 days if clinically appropriate.
  • After CABG — ideally within 28 days and no later than 42 days, considering sternal healing and postoperative complications.
  • After PCI, account for residual ischemia, complications, and vascular access site.
  • Unstable angina, decompensated heart failure, uncontrolled dangerous arrhythmia, active myocarditis/pericarditis, or pathological hemodynamic response exclude unsupervised training.

06 · Load after stroke

Aerobic, strength, functional, and balance training

Aerobic load

ParameterPrescription
FrequencyAt least 3 days per week; light activity on other days too
DurationAt least 20 minutes in training zone; with severe deconditioning — repeated bouts up to 5 minutes
IntensityUsually 40–70% heart rate reserve; in very deconditioned, effect possible from 30%; with β-blockade/AF use Borg and external power
TypeWalking with or without support, partial-weight treadmill, cycle/stepper, arm and leg ergometry, adapted group training
Warm-up/cool-downAt least 3–5 minutes each; longer with orthostasis and deconditioning

Strength, functional, and balance training

ComponentPrescription
Strength work2–3 days/week; 1–3 sets of 10–15 reps; about 50–80% 1RM as tolerated
Functional practiceRepeated task training: sit-to-stand, transfers, stairs, walking, affected arm work
BalanceAt least 2–3 days/week; with falls — structured program with environment and aid assessment
ProgressionSafety and movement quality first, then volume, speed, and resistance

07 · Nutrition

Nutrition and secondary prevention

GoalApproach
Overall patternMediterranean or DASH-like pattern; vegetables, fruit, whole grains, legumes, fish, nuts, unsaturated fats
SodiumRestriction per BP, heart failure, and individual plan
DysphagiaFood texture and fluid thickness only per swallow assessment; ensure adequate energy, protein, and fluids
MalnutritionScreening and individual support; do not prescribe energy deficit before nutritional status is stable
Excess weightGradual loss after stabilization while preserving muscle mass

08 · Monitoring

Monitoring and stopping exercise

Monitor neurological symptoms, BP, HR/rhythm, Borg, pain, fatigue, movement quality, fall risk, and SpO₂/glucose when indicated.

Stop exercise immediately

  • new focal neurological symptoms;
  • sudden severe headache;
  • loss of consciousness;
  • chest pain;
  • marked breathlessness;
  • hemodynamically significant arrhythmia;
  • pathological blood pressure drop;
  • repeat fall;
  • suspected aspiration.
