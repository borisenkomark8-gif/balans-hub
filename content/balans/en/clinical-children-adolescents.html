03 · Strength training

Strength training for children and adolescents

ParameterPrescription
Admission conditionEmotional and motor readiness to follow instructions; qualified supervision; safe equipment
Frequency2–3 days a week, not in a row
DurationAbout 20–30 minutes as part of general preparation
Initial volume1–2 sets of 8–15 reps with technique that allows you to complete the entire set without failure
ProgressionFirst technique and volume, then a slight increase in resistance; exclude sudden load surges
ExercisesAll major muscle groups; deadlift, press, squat/stand, tilt/hip, carry, stabilization and jumping skills by age
Maximum attemptsNot required for beginners; Maximum testing is only permissible using a valid protocol and under qualified supervision
BanNo competition with adult weights, no uncontrolled attempts and no training through acute pain

04 · Nutrition

Nutrition in stages

Age/stagePractical basis
0–6 monthsBreast milk is the preferred option when available; alternatives and supplements - according to pediatric indications and national recommendations
From 6 monthsStart complementary feeding at approximately 6 months with signs of readiness; continued breastfeeding; gradual variety and suitable consistency
6–23 monthsAdequate frequency and variety of meals, iron sources, texture adaptation, responsive feeding; avoid foods with risk of aspiration
2–9 yearsRegular family pattern: vegetables/fruits, whole grains, legumes, dairy or fortified alternatives, sources of protein and unsaturated fats; Minimize free sugars and sugary drinks
10–17 yearsConsider growth spurt, puberty, sports, menstrual function, and risk of low energy availability; do not use aggressive energy deficit
Limitation. Fixed universal calories and “ideal weight” are not published on the page. Energy requirements are determined by growth dynamics, BMI for age, stage of maturation, activity and disease.

06 · Safety

Termination and referral criteria

Stop the exercise and evaluate the child for syncope/presyncope, chest pain, unusual shortness of breath, palpitations with hemodynamic symptoms, neurological deficit, acute trauma, or acute deterioration of tolerance.

A separate referral is required for abnormal growth curve, unintentional weight loss, delayed puberty, amenorrhea, repeated stress injuries, suspected eating disorder, or family history of sudden death.
