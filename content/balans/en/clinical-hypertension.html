Heart and vessels

Hypertension

A practical flow: confirm the baseline, agree treatment goals, and gradually build nutrition, movement, and self-monitoring. Lifestyle changes support prescribed therapy; they do not replace it.

Where to start Nutrition Physical activity Monitoring When to seek urgent care

01 · Baseline

Where to start

Measurements

Check technique

Use a properly sized cuff and measure blood pressure at rest. Home readings help, but diagnosis and cardiovascular risk are assessed by a clinician.

Context

Account for comorbidities

Diabetes, kidney or heart disease, pregnancy, medicines, and symptoms change targets and safe activity levels.

Treatment

Do not change it on your own

Even when you feel well, do not stop or adjust medicines without discussing it with your doctor.

02 · Nutrition

Key principles

Build the base of the diet

  • Vegetables, fruit, legumes, and whole grains.
  • Protein sources with moderate saturated fat.
  • Less highly processed food, cured meats, salty snacks, and ready-made sauces.
  • If overweight — a steady, moderate energy deficit rather than extreme diets.

Watch sodium

WHO guidance for adults is under 2000 mg sodium per day (under 5 g salt). This is a population reference; individual limits depend on health and treatment.

Much sodium comes not from the salt shaker but from bread, cheese, processed meat, convenience foods, and sauces.

03 · Movement

Aerobic and strength work

Aerobic work150 minmoderate intensity per week — general WHO reference
Strength work2+ daysper week with gradual volume increase
Starting pointAs toleratedespecially after a break or with uncontrolled pressure
During strength exercises: keep controlled breathing, avoid maximal efforts without prior assessment, and stop if you have chest pain, marked breathlessness, dizziness, or unusual worsening of symptoms.

04 · Follow-up

What to track

Blood pressure and pulse. Record values with date, time, and how you feel.

Medicines. Note missed doses and side effects to discuss with your doctor.

Lifestyle. Track activity, weight, nutrition, and sleep — trends matter more than one perfect day.

When urgent care is needed

Very high blood pressure and new symptoms

With blood pressure around 180/120 mmHg or higher plus chest pain, severe breathlessness, severe headache, vision changes, confusion, weakness, or numbness, seek emergency care.
