Nutrition and metabolism

Energy balance

The body needs energy for movement, maintaining temperature, organ function and chemical reactions.

What energy expenditure is made of

  • BMR — basal metabolic rate at rest.
  • NEAT — everyday movement outside workouts.
  • TEF — energy spent digesting and absorbing food.
  • Training — planned physical activity.

The practical principle

  • With a sustained energy surplus, body weight usually rises.
  • With a sustained deficit, it falls.
  • At balance, it stays roughly stable on average.
Weight does not change only because of fat. The scale reflects water, glycogen, salt, gut contents, stress, training and hormonal fluctuations. That is why the trend over several weeks matters more.

Key takeaway: lasting results are built on enough protein, structured strength training and an average energy balance over the week.

Nutrition: a practical foundation

What to build a diet from

There is no single menu for everyone. But there are guiding principles: adequate intake, variety, moderation and balance — adjusted for age, activity, goals and health status.

01Proteinmuscle · recovery · satiety

Protein provides amino acids for muscles, enzymes, hormones, the immune system and tissue repair. Both the total amount per day and the regular presence of protein foods at main meals matter.

Practical guideline

  • For most adults, a varied diet covers the basic requirement.
  • With regular strength training, a practical guideline for a healthy adult is often about 1.6 g/kg of body weight per day.
  • More is not necessarily better: the target depends on training load, energy deficit, age and body composition.

Sources

  • Fish, poultry, meat, eggs, dairy products.
  • Legumes, soy, tofu, nuts and seeds.
  • On a plant-based diet, it helps to combine different sources over the course of the day.

With kidney or liver disease, pregnancy and other special conditions, it is better to agree on the amount of protein with a doctor.

02Fatsenergy · cells · vitamins

Fats are needed for cell membranes, absorption of vitamins A, D, E and K, synthesis of signaling molecules and energy. Not only the share of fat matters, but also its quality.

Choose more often

  • Olive and canola oils.
  • Fish, nuts, seeds, avocado.
  • A variety of sources instead of one “perfect” food.

Limit

  • Industrial trans fats.
  • Excess saturated fat.
  • Foods where a lot of fat is combined with excess salt or free sugars.

For an adult, fats often make up about 25–30% of dietary energy, but this is not a rigid norm for everyone and not a goal in itself.

03Carbohydratesenergy · movement · training

Carbohydrates are a convenient source of energy for the brain, daily activity and intense muscle work. The amount can vary; the quality of sources usually matters more than banning a whole food group.

The foundation

  • Whole grains and cereal products.
  • Vegetables, fruit and legumes.
  • Potatoes and other starchy foods in a suitable portion.

Do not confuse

  • Sugar does not become “poison,” but free sugars are easy to overconsume.
  • The glycemic index does not describe a whole meal.
  • Before and after training, tolerance, volume and the overall structure of the day matter.

Carbohydrates do not have to be cut automatically when losing weight: what matters is a sustained energy deficit and the quality of the diet.

04Fibersatiety · gut · microbiota

Fiber consists of the indigestible components of plant foods. Soluble types form a gel and are fermented by the microbiota; insoluble types add bulk to the gut contents.

Where to find it

  • Vegetables, fruit and berries.
  • Legumes, oats, whole grains.
  • Nuts and seeds; if needed, psyllium with enough fluid.

How to increase it

  • Gradually, not in a single day.
  • With enough to drink.
  • Guided by tolerance: bloating is a reason to take a smaller step, not to give up for good.

A practical guideline for adults is about 25–30 g of fiber per day, mainly from food.

05Supplementsfor a reason, not “just in case”

Supplements can cover a confirmed deficiency or a special need, but they do not replace varied nutrition, sleep, physical activity and treatment.

Before buying

  • Define the goal: which deficiency or outcome do you want to change?
  • Check the evidence, dose and duration.
  • Account for medications and other supplements you already take.

Red flags

  • Promises of “detox,” curing many diseases or rapid fat burning.
  • Hidden composition and “proprietary blends” without doses.
  • Megadoses of vitamins and minerals without an indication.

Supplements can cause side effects and interact with medications. It is especially important to discuss them with a doctor during pregnancy, in children, before surgery and with chronic conditions.

06Designing a dietstarting from real life, not an ideal menu

A workable diet is one that matches the goal and stays doable for weeks and months. Start with the structure, and fine-tune exact grams and meal timing later.

1Define the goalWeight maintenance, weight loss, muscle gain, well-being or a medical objective.
2Build the baseA protein food, vegetables or fruit, a source of complex carbohydrates and a moderate amount of fat.
3Choose a rhythm3–5 meals are usually convenient, but the schedule should suit your day and appetite control.
4Check the trendAssess hunger, energy, training, digestion and weight change over several weeks.
5Change one thing at a timeA small adjustment, observation and only then the next step.

The simple plate: roughly half vegetables, a quarter protein food, a quarter side dish; fats and portion size are adjusted to the person.

Physiology and behavior

Why knowing is not enough

Appetite, energy levels and behavior change under the influence of physiology, environment and habits. This does not cancel personal choice — but it helps build a system that requires less constant struggle with yourself.

01Appetite and satietybrain · gut · adipose tissue · environment

Hunger is not a single signal and not a lack of willpower. The brain integrates information about energy stores, stomach fullness, food composition, sleep, stress, habits and food availability.

What is involved

  • Leptin reflects long-term energy stores; as fat mass falls, its signal usually weakens.
  • Ghrelin contributes to the onset of hunger and often rises before a meal.
  • PYY and GLP-1 are part of the satiety signaling system after eating.

What helps in practice

  • Protein and high-fiber foods at main meals.
  • Food with lower energy density and sufficient volume.
  • A predictable routine, normal sleep and no overly strict restrictions.

There is no need to try to “fix the hunger hormones” with a single supplement or food. What works is a combination of meal structure, recovery and environment.

02Insulin and glucosenormal physiology without demonizing

Insulin is a vital hormone of the pancreas. It helps regulate glucose levels, takes part in delivering nutrients to tissues and in replenishing glycogen stores.

MYTH

Any rise in insulin automatically turns food into fat.

MORE ACCURATELY

A normal response after eating is physiological. A sustained increase in fat mass is determined primarily by a prolonged energy surplus.

MYTH

To keep insulin “level,” you have to eliminate carbohydrates completely.

MORE ACCURATELY

Carbohydrates can come from whole grains, vegetables, fruit and legumes; the amount is chosen to fit health, activity and tolerance.

Supporting insulin sensitivity

Regular movement and strength training, enough sleep, a fiber-rich diet and reducing excess body weight — when it is genuinely indicated.

With diabetes, prediabetes, pregnancy or use of glucose-lowering medications, nutrition and exercise recommendations should be individualized.

03Adaptation during weight losswhy progress may slow

As weight falls, energy expenditure decreases: a smaller body requires less energy, the thermic effect of food declines, and a person may unknowingly move less. An additional adaptive component is also possible.

A plateau is not a breakdown

  • Weight is masked by water, salt, glycogen and gut contents.
  • Look at the average trend, not a single weigh-in.
  • Check changes over 2–4 weeks under comparable conditions.

What to check

  • Whether steps and daily activity have been maintained.
  • Whether portions and unplanned snacks have crept up.
  • Whether protein, sleep and strength training are sufficient to preserve muscle.

Do not start with an even harsher deficit. First separate adaptation from ordinary weight fluctuations and behavior changes.

04Self-controlenvironment beats daily heroics

Fatigue, stress, lack of sleep and a multitude of decisions make impulsive choices more likely. That is why a sustainable plan relies not on constant effort but on an environment prepared in advance.

Make it easier

Keep suitable food visible and ready; choose your workout time in advance; pack your gear the evening before.

Add a pause

Do not keep trigger foods in plain sight; turn off unnecessary delivery notifications; go to the store with a list.

Drop “all or nothing”

One unplanned meal does not cancel the day. The next action simply brings you back to the usual plan.

05Motivation and habitsa specific action instead of an abstract wish

Motivation fluctuates. It is more reliable to link a situation to a specific action in advance and make the minimum version simple enough for a bad day.

FORMULA

If it is Monday, 6:00 PM, then I change clothes and start the first five minutes of a workout.

Make the behavior specific

  • Not “move more,” but “walk for 10 minutes after lunch.”
  • Not “eat right,” but “add a protein food to breakfast.”

Create feedback

  • Track whether you did it, not just your weight.
  • Review the plan once a week and change one element at a time.

Special topics

Where context matters

Some topics cannot be reduced to a single number or a universal rule. Here the goal, symptoms, individual response and health status matter especially.

01Micronutrientsvitamins · minerals · electrolytes

Vitamins and minerals are needed in small amounts but take part in nearly every system of the body. The best first step is varied nutrition; supplements are for a confirmed deficiency, increased requirements or a restricted diet.

Vitamin D

Depends on sun exposure, season, diet and individual factors. It is reasonable to choose a supplement dose based on risk and medical advice, not on the principle of “the more, the better.”

Iron

The risk of deficiency is higher with menstrual blood loss, pregnancy, a restrictive diet and some diseases. Iron should not be taken blindly.

Vitamin B12

Especially important on a vegan diet and in conditions with impaired absorption. Neurological signs of deficiency should not be put off “until a convenient test.”

Iodine, calcium, magnesium

Assessed in the context of diet, medications, thyroid and kidney function and other conditions. Symptoms such as fatigue or cramps are nonspecific.

Multivitamins are not insurance against an unbalanced diet. Megadoses and combining several supplements increase the risk of exceeding the safe upper level.

02Ketogenic dieta tool, not a mandatory step

A ketogenic diet is a very-low-carbohydrate, high-fat eating pattern that increases the production of ketone bodies. It is not “zero carbs” and not a separate law of weight loss.

What is important to understand

  • Early on, weight often drops quickly, partly due to loss of water and glycogen.
  • For changing fat mass, energy balance, protein and adherence to the plan still matter.
  • With intense training, some people find it harder to maintain their usual power and volume.

Practical limitations

  • It is harder to get enough fiber and variety without a well-planned menu.
  • Constipation, headache, weakness and changes in the lipid profile are possible.
  • Exogenous ketones and MCT oil do not automatically turn a diet into an effective weight-loss plan.
The clinical ketogenic diet is used, among other things, for some forms of drug-resistant epilepsy — as a medical protocol under the supervision of a team of specialists. Ketosis should not be confused with dangerous ketoacidosis.

A ketogenic diet should not be started on your own with type 1 diabetes, pregnancy or eating disorders. With diseases of the pancreas, liver, kidneys and gallbladder, it must be discussed with a doctor beforehand.

03Female physiology and trainingindividual response instead of a calendar template

Women can increase strength and muscle mass comparably to men in relative terms. The basic principles of a program are the same: appropriate load, progression, recovery and adequate nutrition.

NOT NEEDED

Automatically restructuring every training week solely by calendar phase.

MORE USEFUL

Tracking your own symptoms, performance, sleep and recovery over several cycles.

POSSIBLE

On certain days, changing volume, intensity or exercise selection because of pain, fatigue or how you feel.

IMPORTANT

Not treating missed periods as a normal price for low body fat or a high training volume.

Universal “cycle-based periodization” has not yet been reliably confirmed. Persistent changes in the cycle, unusual bleeding, severe pain or signs of low energy availability call for medical evaluation.

04Pain and training injuriesload management · returning to movement

Pain does not always accurately reflect the degree of damage: it is influenced by tissue condition, sleep, stress, fear and past experience. But that does not mean any pain should be ignored.

If there are no warning signs

  • Remove or modify the movement that sharply increases symptoms.
  • Keep up tolerable activity and train the unaffected areas.
  • Gradually restore range of motion, resistance, volume and then speed.
  • Judge the response not only during the load but also the next day.

In-person evaluation is needed

  • Deformity, rapid marked swelling or large bruising.
  • Inability to bear weight on the limb or noticeable loss of function.
  • Numbness, progressive weakness or loss of bladder or bowel control.
  • Fever, worsening general condition or progression of symptoms.
1Tolerable movements2Controlled load3Usual volume4Sport-specific load

The BALANS principle: work around the problem rather than through a sharp worsening. Complete rest is needed less often than a sensible temporary change in load.

Health and recovery

Sleep

Sleep quality depends on light, temperature, bedroom conditions, routine and the level of mental tension.

01

Light

In the evening, reduce bright lighting and screen light. At night, aim for a bedroom that is as dark as possible.

02

Temperature and air

A cool, well-ventilated bedroom usually helps with falling asleep and staying asleep.

03

Mattress and pillow

Choose support based on comfort, body position and the absence of morning pain, not on an advertising name.

04

Offloading your thoughts

Before bed, write down unfinished tasks and tomorrow’s plan — this reduces the need to hold everything in working memory.
